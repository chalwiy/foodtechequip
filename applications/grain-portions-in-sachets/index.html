Portioning Grains for Roll-Film Sachets or Supplied Packs

A grain portion can be delivered into a pack you supply or form part of a roll-film sachet operation. Choose that route first, then discuss how the required portion is defined and checked.

Roll-film sachets are a complete packaging task

The cup-sachet and granule-sachet products include film handling as well as material feeding. Describe the finished sachet, not just the grains. Film construction, finished dimensions and portion belong in the same brief because they define what the machine is being asked to produce.

Separate dosing starts with the receiving pack

The granule weighing-and-dosing product offers several named configurations. Show the bag or container receiving each portion and explain support at the outlet. If sealing is also needed, identify the separate closure step rather than assuming that dispensing includes a finished bag.

Volume and weight need different comparisons

A cup portion is described by occupied volume; a weight target needs a defined checking requirement. Bulk density helps relate the two for a particular grain sample. Keep the normal material condition and target units clear, including variation in grain size or settling that matters to your process.

Plan the work around portioning

Show how grains reach the hopper and how finished packs are collected. Compare rates only for the intended portion and named configuration. Include loading and collection in the output discussion so an individual machine figure is not treated as the performance of an entire packing operation.
