Garment Compression Packaging: Starting Size, Finished Pack and Recovery

For garment compression packaging, compare the starting pack, the intended compressed shape and what happens after reopening. This is a non-food volume-reduction task, separate from food-preservation packaging.

Compression and evacuation describe different actions

Mechanical compression changes the space occupied by a compressible load; evacuation removes air from the package. They can be combined, but the result depends on the product and packaging. Explain which result is required rather than treating every vacuum layout as a compression machine.

Measure the starting and intended pack

Record the actual folded garments and bag as they will be loaded, including uneven areas or accessories. Provide the desired finished envelope separately. A bag-width figure and the outside dimensions of the machine answer different questions from the size of the loaded pack and its working clearance.

Include response after reopening

Describe any requirements for appearance and shape after the pack is opened. Use representative garments and the planned storage conditions when discussing evaluation. Do not infer a universal reduction ratio or recovery result from an image of compressed clothing; the garment construction and intended handling need their own assessment.

Treat the bag and handling as part of the task

Supply the intended film construction and bag-mouth arrangement together with the garment sample. Explain loading, support, removal and collection. The listed garment compression unit has an overhead pneumatic frame; discuss the complete site and operating requirements for that configuration instead of deriving utility or protection features from its appearance.
