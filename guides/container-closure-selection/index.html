Matching Containers, Closures and Sealing Equipment

Provide the container and its matching cap, liner or end, together with dimensions and the required closing result. Evaluate the components and equipment as one packaging task.

Capping and induction sealing are different operations

Applying a closure mechanically and sealing a foil liner address different parts of the package. For induction sealing, the liner and container material must form a suitable combination. Provide the complete set and describe how containers are presented to the sealing head.

Can bodies require matching ends

Provide the can-and-end supplier’s format drawings and seam requirements. Define how the finished double seam will be examined and measured for that container format.

Identify the jar finish and station arrangement

For vacuum jar capping, include jar finish, closure construction and loading sequence. Compare electrical and dimensional information for the selected station arrangement.

Use a complete enquiry sample

Send a container drawing, matching closure and the requested closing result. Where relevant, include liner or gasket details and how the finished closure will be evaluated. Discuss the intended product separately from the closure mechanics.

Describe the order of closing operations

Show the container as it arrives and after each requested operation. Specify whether caps are already fitted, whether a liner is present and where closed containers go next. Keeping these handovers explicit helps avoid treating capping, liner sealing and finished-closure assessment as one interchangeable operation.
