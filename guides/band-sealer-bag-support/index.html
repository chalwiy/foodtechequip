Band Sealer Selection: Filled Bag Height, Weight and Support

Select a band sealer around the filled bag and the way it enters the sealing section. Flat bag dimensions alone do not explain support, orientation or operator handling.

Measure a filled sample

Record bag width, filled height, thickness and weight with the normal product inside. Show the mouth area available above the contents, including gussets or folds. Mark the intended seal position and explain whether the product must stay upright. A photograph with dimensions helps connect the bag drawing to the loading task.

Distinguish orientation from automation

Continuous band sealers are available in different orientations. The relevant question is how the actual filled bag rests while its mouth passes through the sealing area. Compare loading method, conveyor support and pouch compatibility for the specific vertical configuration. Check the chosen equipment and bag together.

Include the work before and after sealing

Describe who presents the bag mouth, how the base is supported and where the finished bag is collected. If weighing happens immediately before sealing, show the transfer between the two tasks. A quoted belt movement or machine rate cannot by itself establish the number of filled bags an operator can handle.

Agree material and seal evaluation

Send the bag construction and supplier information, not just the word plastic. Describe any contamination likely near the mouth and the closure result required. Ask how representative samples should be evaluated for the proposed configuration. Do not treat an attractive seal appearance as proof of shelf life or food safety.
