Ultrasonic Bag Sealing: Material Layers and Joint Design

For ultrasonic bag sealing, the material at the joint matters as much as the outside bag size. Describe the complete layer structure and where the working head can reach it.

What creates the joint

Ultrasonic sealing uses high-frequency mechanical motion to generate local heat at a compatible joining area. In packaging, thermoplastic content and the layers in contact are central questions. A material described as fabric or nonwoven can have different compositions, so a bag label alone is not a material-compatibility assessment.

Separate material suitability from machine layout

The FTE ultrasonic-bag range includes records for nonwoven, woven and plastic-bag tasks. Use the material description of the chosen product as a starting point, then compare a real sample. Joint access, support and positioning still need to be considered even when the material family appears relevant.

Show the entire seam area

Mark the desired seam, folded mouth, gussets and any transitions in thickness. Include the layers that will actually overlap, with coatings or inserts. This gives a clearer basis for discussing the working head and intended joint than sending only a flat swatch from the middle of a bag.

Evaluate the required result

State the closure result and how it will be assessed on the intended filled pack. Include any residue that normally reaches the joint as a condition to discuss. Evaluate the actual joint and material under the normal conditions of your packaging task.
