Can Seamer Selection: Body, End and Format Change

Select a can seamer using a matching can body and end, not can diameter alone. The format, supporting arrangement and required seam evaluation belong in the same equipment brief.

Why a matching end matters

Can seaming joins the end to the body at their formed edges. A body and an end with similar nominal diameters are not a substitute for the matching format. Obtain both drawings and samples from the packaging supplier, including the supplier’s seam requirements. The machine enquiry can then refer to one defined package.

Read rotating and non-rotating configurations separately

The FTE can-seaming range includes rotating and non-rotating configurations as well as tabletop and inline equipment. Explain how the filled contents and container should be handled during closing. Compare only the height, diameter and rate belonging to the chosen configuration; the largest range from each row should not be combined.

Define each format change

List every body-and-end combination rather than only the smallest and largest diameter. Ask which format-dependent parts, setup steps and checks the quotation includes. A stated diameter range does not mean that every end within it shares identical tooling or that a format change is automatic.

Separate seam evaluation from production planning

Define who will assess the completed seam and which packaging-supplier requirements apply. Also show how a can is presented, supported and removed. The result is a brief covering the package, the setup and the handling task, without treating a listed cycle rate as the finished output of an entire filling-and-closing operation.
