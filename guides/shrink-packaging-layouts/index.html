Chamber, Sealing-and-Tunnel or Sleeve Shrink Packaging?

Compare shrink packaging layouts by the package you want to make and the steps the equipment must perform. Film closing and film shrinking are distinct tasks; a tunnel alone is only one part of that sequence.

Start with the finished film format

A film-wrapped individual item and a sleeve-wrapped bundle can have different openings and presentation. Sketch the required finished pack, including any tray, rather than selecting a machine from product dimensions alone. Ask whether the proposed sealing stage produces that format with the intended film.

Compare where sealing and shrinking happen

A chamber layout brings the working area into one enclosure. A separate sealing-and-tunnel arrangement moves the wrapped item between stages. The FTE range includes a chamber shrink unit, film-sealing and shrink lines, a sleeve line and a standalone tunnel. Compare those actual layouts; the catalogue does not require you to treat a tunnel as a complete wrapping machine.

Distinguish film width from package clearance

Film width describes a consumable dimension. The clearance needed by a loaded pack is a different question involving its width, height, support and travel direction. Show the complete item or grouped bundle to discuss the working envelope. Keep each configuration’s figures together rather than borrowing a width or speed from another row.

Include product handling and heat exposure

Identify which loading, sealing and collection steps are manual and where the pack is supported. Discuss the intended film and how the package should tolerate the shrinking stage. Product appearance, film fit and handling should be evaluated on the same pack; a broad equipment label cannot define every film or product combination.
