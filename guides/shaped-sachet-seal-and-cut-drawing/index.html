Shaped Sachets: Separate the Fill Area, Seal and Cut Outline

A shaped-sachet outline is only part of a packaging specification. Show the filled area, surrounding seal and final cut contour separately so the proposed pack can be discussed as a working package.

Different outlines answer different questions

The outer silhouette describes the finished appearance. The seal boundary describes where layers join, while the space inside must accommodate the intended dose. Keeping those outlines distinct helps explain whether a narrow neck, corner or curved edge is decorative, functional or part of the closing area.

Describe the fill before approving artwork

Show the target dose and liquid or sauce condition at filling, including relevant particles and consistency. The occupied volume belongs inside the proposed pack, with room for the required seal. Compare usable fill quantity with the internal area, rather than the outside dimensions alone. Discuss the material and film together with the shape.

Treat opening and print requests explicitly

If a tear feature, pouring point, print position or alignment is important, mark it as a requirement on the drawing. Discuss whether and how the proposed configuration can provide it. A shaped-sachet product name does not automatically include a spout, a tear notch, print registration or an unlimited choice of contours.

Identify the pack and format scope

The FTE shaped-sachet machine is film-fed vertical equipment with a forming section. Supply the finished drawing and film specification for that configuration. If several shapes are needed, list them separately and ask what format-dependent setup is involved. Clarify whether the enquiry concerns equipment or packaging consumables; those are separate supply requests.
