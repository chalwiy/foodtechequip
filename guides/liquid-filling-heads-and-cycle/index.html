Liquid Filling Output: Compare Head Count and the Whole Cycle

Head count describes the filling arrangement, not the complete output of a workcell. Compare machines at the same fill quantity and container format, including the time and actions needed before and after dispensing.

Distinguish a head from a finished bottle

A filling head delivers material at a working position. A finished bottle also has to arrive, be presented correctly and leave that position. Ask how the heads operate and how containers are positioned in the proposed configuration. Head count alone does not describe whether those operations occur together or in sequence.

Compare matched configurations

The FTE range includes a compact ceramic-pump unit, a four-head configuration and a twin-head pump arrangement with a conveyor. Use the corresponding product row and intended bottle rather than combining a rate from one configuration with a head count from another. Keep the liquid condition and target fill identical in a meaningful comparison.

Include the container-handling cycle

Where an operator presents containers, include positioning, removal and access to the next empty bottle. Where a conveyor presents them, describe arrival spacing, support and collection. The quoted equipment should make clear which movements and controls it provides and which remain operator tasks or separate handling requirements.

State output for the actual operating task

Give the required finished output at the intended quantity, not only a preferred number of heads. Explain material replenishment, planned pauses and the following closure step. A comparison can then consider filling and handling together, without assuming that multiplying a nozzle figure by the number of heads predicts the whole process.
