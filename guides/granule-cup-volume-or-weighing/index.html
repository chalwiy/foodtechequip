Granule Portions: Volumetric Cups or a Weighing Task?

Compare granule equipment using two decisions: how the portion is defined and how it reaches the package. A cup measures a volume; a weighing task is defined by mass. Neither decision, by itself, specifies the complete bagging operation.

Compare the measurement basis first

A volumetric cup defines the space occupied by a portion. The mass occupying that space depends on the material and its bulk density. Where a target weight is important, explain how it will be checked and what variation your process accepts. Keep millilitres and grams separate when comparing quotations.

Separate portioning from producing the sachet

The FTE cup-sachet product combines cup configurations with film handling. A separate granule weighing-and-dosing machine dispenses into a receiving pack or container that must be described. Compare the complete scope: film supply, pack formation, portion delivery, closure and collection. A dosing quote and a finished-sachet quote cover different operations.

Read the cup configurations independently

The cup-sachet record contains large-cup, small-cup, swing-cup and 320C configurations. Use the portion and rate associated with the configuration being discussed. Explain the intended grain size and normal material condition. Do not convert a cup-volume label into a universal package weight or combine the fastest rate with another configuration’s portion.

Define a meaningful comparison

Use the same material and intended pack when comparing routes. Record the target portion, how portions are collected and checked, and any change after handling or settling. If a weight-based control is requested, make its function explicit in the quotation. A machine name alone is not a description of every checking or feedback feature.
