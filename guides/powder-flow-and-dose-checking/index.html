Powder Dosing: Flow Behaviour and Fill-Weight Checks

A powder name is not enough to select a dosing arrangement. Describe how the material feeds, the portion required and how each dose will be collected and checked.

Record what reaches the hopper

Include the normal powder condition, any visible lumps or fines and whether it clings during transfer. Explain how the hopper is replenished and whether material has recently been mixed or stored. A representative production sample and your own handling observations are more useful than assuming all powders with the same ingredient name behave alike.

Keep volume and weight checks separate

Bulk density links a powder volume with its mass. Changes in settling can therefore affect the weight occupying a given volume. Specify how you need to check the dose and whether weight feedback forms part of the requested control task. State the target portion and the variation your process can accept.

Compare the actual dosing task

The catalogue includes an auger powder doser with a hopper and discharge section, and separate weighing and dosing configurations. Decide whether the quotation concerns dispensing alone, weighing, bag presentation or closing as well. Read only the specifications of the selected configuration; a label such as powder filler does not define every control function.

Describe the pack and the checking sequence

Provide the narrowest receiving opening, the support below the outlet and what the operator does between portions. Explain where the empty pack weight is accounted for and where the filled portion is checked. If a machine trial is required, agree its material, pack format, running conditions and acceptance criteria before interpreting results.
