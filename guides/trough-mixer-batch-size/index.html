Trough Mixer Capacity: Litres, Batch Weight and Cycle Time

Choose a batch mixer using the intended charge and mixing result, not vessel volume alone. Litres describe the trough; kilograms describe the material loaded. Both are needed to discuss a CH model.

Why litres and kilograms are different

Working batch weight depends on the material as well as the trough volume. Ask how much space your intended charge occupies in its normal condition, including any change after settling. Share bulk density with its measurement conditions when available. Do not use a water-based conversion to treat a litre of powder as a kilogram.

Read the CH comparison by model

The CH range contains CH-10, CH-20, CH-50, CH-100 and CH-200. Compare the model row as a whole: motor power, voltage, trough volume, listed maximum charge and installation dimensions belong together. For CH-10 and CH-20, send your target batch weight to discuss the working charge separately from trough volume.

Count the complete batch cycle

Assess output using the whole batch cycle. Loading, mixing, emptying and preparation for the next batch all occupy time. Describe the result required and how you intend to assess it, then include the handling steps in your output requirement. Do not assume multiplying batches per hour by a listed charge establishes achievable production.

Plan the work around the trough

Show how ingredients reach the lid, where operators stand and what receives the discharged batch. The larger panel layout differs from the smaller models. Discuss controls and emptying for the chosen model, along with power supply, access and the cleaning requirements of your formulation. Stainless steel housing alone does not specify every product-contact material or a cleaning method.
